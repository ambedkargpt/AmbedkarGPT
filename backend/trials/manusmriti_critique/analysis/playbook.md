## 3. The patterns, and what each becomes on X

**name the verse** (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- Name the text's own address (chapter and verse) and say plainly what it prescribes. It is the single move that makes a viewer able to check; videos that paraphrase without a verse (history explainer, interview, Bengali polemic, the 42-second short) are the ones the analyses mark as least checkable.
- *On X:* Every post names Manusmriti X.Y and quotes a short exact phrase.

**side by side** (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- Put two rules next to each other so the unfairness shows without commentary: the same offence, a different penalty by birth, as a ladder with the real numbers (the verse-by-verse video opens on 100, 150 and a beating for the lower classes against 50, 25 and 12 for a Brahmin). The strongest moment in the shortest critical video as well.
- *On X:* Use the mildest and the harshest case the verses give; 8.268 against 8.270 is the model.

**reverse the roles** (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`)
- Flip the roles in one line ('now reverse it') so the viewer feels the asymmetry instead of being told it.
- *On X:* Two lines and a turn: rule for one party, 'Now reverse the roles.', rule for the mirror case.

**concrete detail** (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- Use the physical or numerical detail, not 'harsh punishments': the length of the nail, the oil in the ears, twelve against two hundred. The most common method of all.
- *On X:* Copy the verse's own detail word for word; never add one.

**stair step** (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`)
- Escalate with a signpost ('and it does not stop there', 'even worse') so a list of rules feels like a climb.
- *On X:* Two or three consecutive verses, one line each, a count at the end.

**not me the book** (seen in `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- Distance the speaker from the claim: it is the book saying it, not the speaker. The verse-by-verse video uses 'I am not saying this' only three times in thirty minutes, always at the sharpest line, and otherwise reports each rule as plain reading. Citing the believer's own authority with an exact location stops it being waved off as an outsider's attack.
- *On X:* Lead with the exact words in quotation marks, then 'That is Manusmriti X.Y, not a paraphrase.'

**target the rule** (seen in `ZokcgYzBVuc`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`)
- Say what is being criticised: the rules and practices, not a faith or its people. The 42-second short does it in one sentence; the debate's best line does it as a definition; the interview condemns every text placed above the Constitution in the same breath to blunt 'only one religion'.
- *On X:* Say 'this verse' and 'this rule', never 'Hinduism' or 'Hindus'. An optional closer states the principle for every text.

**close on a value** (seen in `ZokcgYzBVuc`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `ljPhxi5-140`)
- End on a value or a question (humanity, equal dignity, the law versus the heart), not on a taunt or a call to subscribe. Most of the videos close on a moral statement.
- *On X:* One short closing question or one-line judgement, plainly an opinion.

**start from one scene** (seen in `ljPhxi5-140`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`)
- Begin from one concrete situation instead of a thesis: a night, a riverbank, a farmer wanting to marry off his daughter. It works when the scene is the rule's own; the analyses flag the invented details as a risk.
- *On X:* Open on the situation the verse itself legislates (a Sudra teaches a Brahmana his duty) and add nothing invented.

**concede then object** (seen in `bMTSzFIUbT4`)
- The one counter-critique video wins its audience by granting the critics their strongest point and claiming to have read the text before it pivots. A critical post borrows only the credential: quoting the text exactly is how it shows it has read it.
- *On X:* No concession line; the exact quotation does the job.

**history and authority** (seen in `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `ZokcgYzBVuc`)

- *On X:* Left out unless a fact is supplied as approved context (sources/approved_context.json).

**plain restatement** (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- After each citation the verse-by-verse video restates the rule in one plain line ('that is...'), a takeaway a viewer can repeat. It is the step between a quotation and a verdict.
- *On X:* After the quoted words, one short plain line saying who the rule applies to and what it allows or forbids, using only the verse's words.

**harshest last** (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`, `gmGq3B6jGqA`)
- Rules are grouped by theme and each group ends on its most extreme detail, announced by a calm marker ('interestingly', 'it even goes as far as writing'). The calm is part of the effect; the escalation formula in the short video does the same job.

**claim then verse** (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`)
- A defender's claim is put in plain words and answered with the single verse that contradicts it, with no insult to the person who makes it ('the text has no birth-based discrimination', answered with the verse that makes a freed Sudra still a servant by birth).
- *On X:* The claim_vs_verse pattern: a common claim stated without quotation marks and attributed to nobody, then the exact verse that contradicts it.

**state the scope honestly** (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`)
- Visible concessions make the case harder to dismiss: who else a penalty covers, that other classes fare better than Sudras, that the text also honours women. The verse-by-verse video builds these in and the counter-critique video borrows the same credibility device.
- *On X:* Say exactly who a rule applies to and keep every qualifier in the verse ('his Sudra (slave)', 'arrogantly'). A post that states a rule more broadly than the verse does is the easiest to dismiss.

**show the edition** (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- The strongest credibility move in the set: the speaker names the printed editions he read (including the defenders' own), says he cross-checked the harshest rules across them, and uses no outside commentary.
- *On X:* The review sheet shows each verse's text and its page in the supplied PDF. If the translation is confirmed, posts can name it.

## 4. The post structures

### `asymmetry`: Same act, different penalty
Use when two verses deal with the same kind of act (an insult, a theft, a crime) but set a different penalty by birth-class. Seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`.

1. a 3-6 word opening that names the act and the unfairness (for example: Same insult. Different price.)
2. one line: the LIGHTEST penalty the verses give for this act (the smallest amount, or the mildest punishment), naming exactly who it applies to, with its verse reference
3. one line: the HARSHEST penalty the verses give for it, naming exactly who it applies to, with its verse reference. Choose the two cases that differ MOST; a post that shows only mild cases has no contrast
4. a closing question or one-line judgement, clearly the author's opinion

### `reverse_the_roles`: Now reverse the roles
Use when a rule that is mild for one party and severe for the mirror-image case. Seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`.

1. state the rule for the favoured party in one line with its reference
2. say 'Now reverse the roles.' (or similar) and state the mirrored rule with its reference
3. a closing line that names what the contrast shows

### `verse_stack`: It does not stop there
Use when two or three consecutive verses, each setting its own penalty for its own offence. Seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`.

1. an opening line naming what the rules cover in plain words (not a single offence unless every verse is about it)
2. the rules in order, one short line each: that verse's own offence, then its penalty in the verse's own words. Move between them with a signpost about RULES (for example: Another rule:), never one that makes the penalties sound like stages of one punishment
3. a closing line that counts the verses it cited (the count must be right)

### `quote_first`: Read it yourself
Use when one verse whose exact words are striking. Seen in `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`.

1. lead with the exact words in quotation marks
2. give the reference as the source, in a short line (for example: That is Manusmriti 8.417, not a paraphrase.)
3. one line saying who the rule applies to and what it allows or forbids

### `plain_rule`: One rule, stated plainly
Use when a single verse that ranks, restricts or controls a group. Seen in `ZokcgYzBVuc`, `ljPhxi5-140`.

1. an opening that frames what the rule governs (names, work, property, independence)
2. the rule in the verse's own terms, with its reference and a short exact quotation
3. a closing question that asks the reader to judge it

### `scene_hook`: Start from the situation
Use when a verse that legislates a specific situation (who does what to whom). Seen in `ljPhxi5-140`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`.

1. one line stating the situation exactly as the verse sets it up, with no added detail (for example: A Sudra teaches a Brahmana his duty.)
2. the penalty or rule, with its reference and the verse's own words
3. a closing question

### `protected_and_punished`: Who is protected, who is punished
Use when two verses, possibly far apart in the book, one that shields a group and one that punishes another. Seen in `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `ZokcgYzBVuc`.

1. one line: what the first verse protects or allows, with its reference
2. one line: what the second verse inflicts, with its reference
3. a closing line that sets the two side by side without adding a claim

### `claim_vs_verse`: It is said... the text says
Use when a verse directly contradicts a common claim about the text (for example, that it does not rank people by birth, or that women were free). Seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`.

1. one plain sentence stating the common claim, WITHOUT quotation marks and attributed to no group (for example: It is said the text does not rank people by birth.)
2. the verse that contradicts it: its reference and an exact quotation, with every qualifier kept
3. a short closing line: Read it yourself, or a question

Hook shapes: “Same [act]. Different [price].”; “[A short plain fact]. [Its number or detail].”; “Read it yourself.”; “Even [thing] was ranked.”; “[One situation the verse sets up].”; “It is said [a common claim]. [Reference] says:”

Closers: a short question (Who decides what a child is worth at birth?); a one-line judgement marked as opinion (Not balanced justice.); a count of the verses used (Three verses. One page.); the principle for every text (A rule that ranks people by birth deserves to be read this closely, whatever book it is in.); Read it yourself: Manusmriti X.Y.

## 6. What not to copy

- **Collective statements about a community, caste or class ('the savarna society', 'priests are vultures', 'a master plan')**: Unverifiable and it turns a critique of a text into an attack on people. (seen in `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`)
- **Reading a motive off a whole class as fact ('rules written to enslave', 'the only purpose of sati was property')**: A text's rule can be shown; a motive cannot. (seen in `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `ljPhxi5-140`)
- **Attacking another religion, or framing the critique inside a comparison of religions**: It changes the subject and loses the sceptical reader. (seen in `ljPhxi5-140`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`)
- **Whataboutery (answering a question about this text with another community's law)**: It concedes the point and starts a different fight. (seen in `bMTSzFIUbT4`, `aYODcLwTOg0`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`)
- **Abuse, contempt, or reading an opponent's motive off their caste**: Hands the other side the argument. (seen in `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`)
- **Verse numbers, amounts or attributions given from memory (Narada's fines credited to Manu, 'ten to twenty coins', molten lead credited to the wrong text)**: Several videos get these wrong; a single wrong number ends the argument. (seen in `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `ljPhxi5-140`, `bMTSzFIUbT4`)
- **Claims about what was done in practice, or for how long, with no source**: The book prescribes; whether it was applied is a historical claim. (seen in `ljPhxi5-140`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `ZokcgYzBVuc`)
- **Naming modern leaders and organisations as endorsers without a source**: An accusation, not evidence; and it is politics, not the text. (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`, `y8D9SgbsBKg`)
- **Nazi or Fascist parallels, and calls to burn or destroy the book**: Inflammatory and off-topic. (seen in `y8D9SgbsBKg`, `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `myaXbAzq-MQ`)
- **An invented character or scene presented as if it were a case**: If a scene is used it must be the verse's own, and labelled. (seen in `myaXbAzq-MQ`, `ljPhxi5-140`)
- **Forced-admission loops, thumbnail bait and 'subscribe' taunts**: Retention tricks that do not belong in a post. (seen in `YEcZXeC0F9U`, `ZokcgYzBVuc`)
- **Naming a caste group in the hook as reverse psychology**: It works on video; in a post it can read as singling out a community. (seen in `gmGq3B6jGqA`)
- **Sweeping lines about the whole book ('every page and every verse humiliates someone')**: False the moment a single counter-example is found. (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- **Mocking the person who holds the opposite view ('slap in the face' lines)**: Keep the contrast between the verse and the claim, not between you and a person. (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`)
- **Tying a news incident to a verse by placement alone, or implying a publisher removed verses deliberately without evidence**: Correlation by editing, not evidence; and outside the supplied book. (seen in `QS75VNhCOm8`)
